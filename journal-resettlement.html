Reading the map

The space between services matters.

The World Health Organization describes refugee and migrant health in relation to housing, employment, income and access to care. Its mental health evidence review also examines identity, community support, basic needs and navigating services. These perspectives invite a wider view of what makes care accessible. [1] [2]

Our interpretation is that services should examine the connections between their responsibilities. A health referral, for example, might prompt a conversation about language support or the practical next step. This diagram makes those questions visible; it does not establish which intervention works best.

A question for service design

What has to work around a service
for that service to work?

Start with the person. Examine the system.

Ask what people want to preserve, what they want to pursue, and which obstacles they want help addressing. Then examine whether services connect around those priorities. This is the editorial argument of the map, rather than a finding from a new study.

How this visual was developed

This essay draws on two WHO publications. The nodes and links were selected editorially to explain interacting dimensions of resettlement. No individual records, survey data or computational model were used. The sources cover refugees and migrants across settings; they do not describe every refugee’s experience or establish California-specific effects. This is an educational visual essay, not original research or an independently peer-reviewed review.

Sources & further reading
