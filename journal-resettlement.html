01 / The appointment

Care begins before the visit.

An appointment provides a time and place for care. Reaching it may still require clear information, practical arrangements, and support navigating an unfamiliar healthcare system.

02 / Understand the next step

What does this referral mean?

Understanding a referral means knowing where to go and what to do next. Unfamiliar terminology and language barriers can make those instructions difficult to follow. The World Health Organization recommends interpreting, translated resources, and practical assistance navigating care. [1]

03 / Make room for care

How does care fit into daily life?

Attending a visit may involve taking time away from work, arranging childcare, or coordinating other caregiving responsibilities. A conversation about scheduling can help clarify which commitments need to be considered.

04 / Reach the appointment

How do I get there?

Knowing the clinic’s address is one part of the journey. Planning transportation, finding the correct entrance, and allowing enough travel time are practical details that may need attention before the visit.

05 / Connect the steps

Make the next step clearer.

Support could include understandable instructions, a discussion of timing and transportation, and a reliable contact for questions. The final view illustrates how these elements could accompany a referral.

The takeaway

Accessible care begins with understanding.

WHO describes language, administrative, and financial barriers to healthcare, and recommends practical support with registration, appointments, and attendance. [1] For organizations connecting people with care, a referral should prompt a further question: what information or assistance does this person need to take the next step?

Conversations with refugee community members help organizations understand what makes care difficult to reach and which support people find useful. Listening throughout the process helps keep services responsive to the priorities people identify.
